Whether it is at school or at home, during a family outing or an after school activity, problems have a habit of arriving without warning. For a lot of children the hard part is not the problem itself. It is knowing how to react and respond in the moment.

Why problems feel so big in the moment

A child in the middle of a problem is usually managing two things at once: the situation in front of them, and the feeling it has set off. The feeling tends to arrive first and shout loudest. Without something concrete to fall back on, many children get stuck there.

Having a familiar set of steps gives them somewhere to put their attention. It will not make the feeling disappear, but it does give them a handhold while it passes.

Worth knowing

Children rarely learn this in the heat of the moment. The steps are best introduced when everyone is calm, so they are already familiar by the time they are needed.

Where it helps

Keep it somewhere it will be seen. Families often find the same few settings are where it earns its keep.

At school

Playground disagreements, group work, a task that suddenly feels too hard.

At home

Sibling conflict, homework frustration, the small daily negotiations.

On family outings

Unfamiliar places and broken routines, where plans change without warning.

At extracurriculars

Sport, music and clubs, where children manage themselves without you nearby.

Using it well

Introduce it calmly

Walk through it together at a quiet moment, not mid meltdown. Familiarity is what makes it usable later.

Put it where it is visible

The fridge, a bedroom door, a classroom wall. It only works if it is in sight when it is needed.

Use the same words every time

Consistent language across home and school helps the steps stick faster.

Practise on small problems

Confidence builds on the little things long before it holds up under the big ones.
