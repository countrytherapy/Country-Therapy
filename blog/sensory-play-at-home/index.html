Sensory play at home: unlocking imagination with everyday items

You don't need specialised equipment or materials. Common household items can become the perfect tools for exploration and learning.

On this page

  1. Why sensory play matters
  2. 5 DIY activities
  3. Tips and precautions
  4. Benefits by age group
  5. Getting started

At Country Therapy, we recognise the importance of sensory play in supporting sensory processing and overall development in children. Sensory experiences can be easily integrated into daily life, and the best part is that you don't need specialised equipment or materials.

Why sensory play matters

Sensory play helps children develop critical skills, including:

  1. Sensory integration and processing
  2. Fine motor skills and hand eye coordination
  3. Problem solving and critical thinking
  4. Emotional regulation and self awareness

Sensory play also:

  • Enhances creativity and imagination
  • Supports cognitive development and learning
  • Fosters independence and self confidence
  • Encourages social skills and communication

5 DIY sensory activities using household items

Sensory bins

Fill containers with rice, beans, sand, or pasta, and hide small toys or other objects for your child to discover. You can also add different textures like fabric, foam, or water.

Homemade playdough

Mix flour, water, salt, and food colouring to create a tactile experience. Add scents like essential oils or herbs for an extra sensory dimension.

Water play

Fill a container with water and add soap, sponges, and cups for a fun and educational experience.

Sensory bottles

Fill empty bottles with water, oil, sand, or rice, and add food colouring, glitter, or small objects for visual and tactile exploration.

Nature exploration

Collect items from nature, like leaves, rocks, and sticks, and create a sensory bin or collage.

Tips and precautions

Before you start

  • Supervise children during sensory play to ensure safety.
  • Adapt activities to suit your child's individual needs and sensitivities.
  • Encourage open ended exploration and imagination.
  • Set boundaries and clean up expectations.
  • Engage with your child during sensory play to foster bonding and social skills.

Sensory play benefits for different age groups

Infants0 to 12 months

Sensory play helps develop foundational sensory skills, hand eye coordination, and cognitive development.

Toddlers1 to 3 years

Sensory play enhances exploration, fine motor skills, and language development.

Preschoolers3 to 5 years

Sensory play fosters creativity, problem solving, and social skills.

Getting started with sensory play

  1. Begin with simple activities and gradually increase complexity.
  2. Observe your child's interests and preferences.
  3. Incorporate sensory play into daily routines (e.g. bath time, outdoor play).
  4. Make sensory play a family activity to encourage bonding.

By incorporating sensory play into your daily routine, you can support your child's development and foster a lifelong love of learning. At Country Therapy, our occupational therapists are dedicated to helping children reach their full potential.

Get started today

Gather household items, get creative, and watch your child's imagination and development thrive.
